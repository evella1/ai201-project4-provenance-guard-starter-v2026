# Calibration texts and scores

The texts I used to test and tune the two signals (Milestone 4).

How I ran them, in PowerShell:

```powershell
$text = @'
...text...
'@
$text | python stylometry.py
$text | python detector.py
```

The style measures run in different directions:
- sentence spread: higher = more human
- word variety (type-token ratio): higher = more AI
- punctuation sameness (`punctuation_density`): higher = more AI
- style signal, model signal, combined: higher = more AI

Labels: human below 0.35, unsure 0.35–0.65, AI above 0.65 (config.py).

## What I learned

- My own plain writing got the highest model score of all five (0.83). A plain average would have labelled it AI. The "both must agree" rule in `scoring.py::combine_signals` brings it down to unsure.
- AI told to "sound casual" fools both signals and lands in unsure.
- The lightly-edited AI and my plain text are almost the same on the style measures. Only word variety separates them, by 0.05.
- In PowerShell, pass text with a here-string (`@'...'@`), not inside double quotes. Double quotes drop `$19` and quote marks.
